# Evaluation layers, a gateway and tracing: findings (AIENG-64 to AIENG-68)

Run on 17 Oct 2026. Evidence: [the generation run and calibration](2026-10-17-generation.json)
(`gpt-6-luna`, BM25 retrieval over the corpus), [the dashboard](../../dashboard.md) written by the
gate, [a real fallback](../../gateway/runs/2026-10-17-fallback-check.md), and the tests. Design and
hypotheses H45 to H49 were committed before any W11 code: [roadmap-w11.md](../../../docs/roadmap-w11.md).

## The generation layer

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Answering accuracy (answers when it should, declines when it should) | **15/16** |
| Context recall (a relevant document retrieved) | 1.0 |
| Context precision (share of retrieved chunks from relevant documents) | 0.45 |
| Citation precision (share of cited documents that are relevant) | 1.0 |
| Judge faithfulness (v2) | 0.62, at 77% agreement with the labels |

- The one miss: the payments incident (w07) **declined** without its attachments. With the alert
  and the chart in W07 it answered; the text alone was not enough. That is the right behaviour for
  the text it had.
- All three off-corpus incidents and the vague one were declined; both misleading incidents were
  answered from the right service's documents, not the one named; the poisoned runbook was ignored.
- Context precision is low by construction: five chunks are retrieved and most cases have one or
  two relevant documents.

## The judge, calibrated

The labels were written by me (the assistant), by reading each claim against its quotes, and
committed before the judge ran, so they were not anchored on its verdicts. They are not the
user's labels.

| Judge | Agreement on "supported" | kappa | What it did |
|---|---|---|---|
| `judge_claim/v1` | 4/13 | 0.06 | rejected any claim using a detail from the report rather than the quote (the region, the release, the service), even hedged ones |
| `judge_claim/v2`, without the service | 8/13 | 0.27 | facts from the report count as given; but the harness had not shown it the incident's service, and it said so: "the report does not identify the application as orders-api" |
| `judge_claim/v2` | **10/13** | **0.45** | the remaining three disagreements are details in neither quote nor report ("hardcoded", "mutual TLS path", naming checkout-api for an orders-api report); the judge is arguably right on all three |

So my labels were more lenient than a careful reading of the rubric, and the first judge was far
stricter than either. Two of the three changes that moved agreement were not about the judge at
all: one clarified the rubric, the other fixed the harness. I stopped there: tuning the rubric
further against the same 13 labels would only fit it to them. **The judge's faithfulness score is
reported beside its agreement and is not gated.** Relevance agreed 13/13, but both raters said yes
every time, so it measures nothing yet.

## The gate

It runs in CI after the tests: retrieval, workflow routing and security are measured on every run;
generation is read from the recorded run; a prompt changed since that run fails the gate until it
is repeated. The four deliberate regressions each fail it.

## Cost

Generation 16 calls (44,826 / 4,218 tokens, about $0.15); the judge three times (13 calls each,
about $0.13 together); the fallback check one call. About **$0.29** at the assumed price. W05 to
W11: about $1.50 of the $5 cap.

## Limits

Sixteen golden cases and thirteen judged causes are small. The labels are the builder's. The
Langfuse adapter was tested against a fake, not a live instance. The gate cannot judge a new prompt
by itself; it can only insist that someone with a key does.
